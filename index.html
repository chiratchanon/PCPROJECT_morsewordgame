Spell words in Morse with your voice

An Arduino UNO R4 WiFi listens through a microphone, turns short and long sounds into dots and dashes, and sends the finished word over MQTT to a game in your browser.

Play the game Download the Arduino code

How it works

  1. The game shows a word. The web page picks a random word and shows the Morse pattern under each letter as a hint.
  2. You speak it in Morse. A short sound is a dot and a long sound is a dash. A pause ends the letter.
  3. The board decodes and displays it. The letters appear on an LCD. A button deletes the last letter.
  4. You press send. The board publishes the word over MQTT, and the page gives a point if it matches.

Reading Morse from sound

The microphone is read for 50 ms at a time, and the difference between the loudest and quietest readings counts as the volume. Anything above a threshold is a sound, and its length decides what it means.

What you hearMeaning
Sound shorter than 400 msDot (.)
Sound 400 ms or longerDash (-)
Silence of about 0.9 secondsEnd of a letter, so it is decoded

Say a quick “di” for a dot and a held “daaah” for a dash. Claps and taps also work well. All the timings are constants at the top of the sketch.

Software

  1. In the Arduino IDE, install the libraries LiquidCrystal I2C and PubSubClient. WiFi support comes with the R4 board package.
  2. Open morse-board.ino and fill in your WiFi name and password. Give each board its own BOARD_ID.
  3. Upload it, open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, and wait for “Online” on the LCD.
  4. Open the game page, press Connect, and start spelling.

MQTT topics

pc/morse/<board id>/word     the word you send
pc/morse/<board id>/status   online / offline (retained)

The project uses a free public MQTT server, so other people could in theory use the same topic names. For a classroom or event, change the pc/morse prefix to something unique in both the sketch and the game page.

Built with an Arduino UNO R4 WiFi, a MAX4466 microphone, and MQTT over the public HiveMQ broker.
