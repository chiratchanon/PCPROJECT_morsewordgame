About this project

Morse code is a language of two signals, short and long. This project asks what happens if a small board can listen to those signals as sound and read them back as letters. Say a quick “di” for a dot and a held “daaah” for a dash, and the board spells out what you said.

  • Listens through a microphone and turns sound length into dots and dashes.
  • Shows each letter on an LCD, with a button to delete the last one.
  • Sends the finished word over WiFi to an MQTT server.
  • Plays a spelling game: the page picks a word and awards a point for each correct one.
  • Supports several boards, each with its own name and score. The first board to spell the word wins the round.
  • Has a mic switch: a knob turns the microphone off while the screen and buttons keep working.

How it works

Your voice→Arduino→MQTT server→This page

The microphone is read for 50 ms at a time. The gap between the loudest and quietest reading counts as the volume, and anything above a threshold is a sound. Its length decides what it means:

What the board hearsMeaning
Sound shorter than 400 msDot (.)
Sound 400 ms or longerDash (-)
Silence of about 0.9 secondsEnd of a letter, so it is decoded and shown

Claps and taps work well too. An early idea was to tell dots from dashes by pitch, but sound length turned out to be simpler and more consistent.

Software

  1. In the Arduino IDE, install the libraries LiquidCrystal I2C and PubSubClient. WiFi support comes with the R4 board package.
  2. Download morse-board.ino, add your WiFi name and password, and give each board its own BOARD_ID.
  3. Upload it and wait for “Online” on the LCD.
  4. Come back to the Play section, press Connect, and start spelling.

MQTT topics

pc/morse/<board id>/word     the word you send
pc/morse/<board id>/status   online / offline (retained)

This uses a free public MQTT server, so other people could use the same topic names. For a class or event, change the pc/morse prefix to something unique in both the sketch and the CFG line in this page’s script.

Built with an Arduino UNO R4 WiFi, a MAX4466 microphone, and MQTT over the public HiveMQ broker.
